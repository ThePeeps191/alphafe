# Decisions

One line (or a short paragraph) per choice, newest at the bottom.

- **Environment**: Python 3.12 in a project-local `.venv` created with `uv`; nothing installed globally (owner's rule).
- **Dependencies**: torch at runtime; pytest, hypothesis, sympy for tests only. No numpy (torch prints a harmless warning without it, filtered in pytest).
- **Symbol classes**: variables are `x y z t u v w` (universally quantified, substitutable); every other lowercase letter except `f` is a named constant (a witness in a proof, a parameter in an answer) and can never be substituted for.
- **Division in the input language**: only by an integer literal (`x/2`), which is how rational constants are written. Division by expressions is out of scope for v1.
- **Canonical form**: polynomials with `Fraction` coefficients over atoms (variable, named constant, or f(canonical polynomial)), stored as dicts keyed by sorted monomial tuples. Written from scratch; SymPy is used only in tests.
- **Leading monomial**: identities are solved for their most complicated monomial, ranked by (nesting depth of f over variables, structural size, canonical order). See `algebra.mono_rank`.
- **Network term syntax**: prefix (Polish) notation, so constrained decoding only has to count owed operands. Integers above 20 collapse to one lossy token `N` in states.
- **Proof state = reduced identities**: each branch keeps its identities in reduced echelon form over Q (every identity solved for its leading monomial). Adding, subtracting and scaling identities therefore happens automatically inside the checker rather than as explicit moves; an explicit "combine" move could never add information. Swapping variables is a substitution (`sub P: x:=y, y:=x`).
- **Rewriting is automatic**: an identity whose leading monomial is a single atom is used as a rewrite rule everywhere, including inside f(...) (congruence). So "rewrite using a known identity" is not a separate move either; to rewrite with an *instance* of an identity, substitute into it first.
- **Properties are flags with their own rewriting**: odd, even, additive, involution are recognised from their defining identity and then applied to every matching f(...). Involution also sets injective and surjective.
- **One-variable identities are renamed automatically** to the other variables in use (an identity for all x is an identity for all y).
- **Flat proofs, depth-first cases**: a proof is a flat list of moves acting on the first open case; a split replaces the current case by its sub-cases. Facts are branch-local by construction (each case is its own object).
- **Case splits**: only on variable-free facts, with factors re-multiplied by the checker (`split`), or `c = 0` versus `c != 0` for a constant c (`splitnz`). The factoring helper is untrusted; the multiplication check is what counts.
- **Injectivity/surjectivity** are established by syntactic criteria (see `checker.injectivity_variable`, `surjectivity_variable`), not by sub-proofs. Narrower than what a human can do, but easy to trust.
- **Preimages** (`pre t`) only for constant targets t; the witness is a named constant and can never be substituted for. The separate move `onto` covers "f(y) takes every value" when y occurs only inside f(y).
- **Conclude = read off + pin down + verify**: `conclude` takes an identity f(a*x + b) = E, replaces every unknown constant in E by a free parameter, substitutes into the *original* equation and solves the coefficient system exactly. Verification of candidate solutions is part of this move (and repeated in `check`), not a separate proof move; `checker.verify_solution` exposes it on its own.
- **Exact parameter solving only**: `solutions.solve_system` solves linear equations, enumerates rational roots and splits common factors; anything else raises `Unsupported` and the move is rejected. Consequence: answers needing irrational constants (f(x) = sqrt(2) x) are out of reach in v1 and are reported as unsolved, never as "no solutions".
- **"No new information" is a rejection**: a legal move that changes nothing is refused, so proofs have no dead steps and search can prune.
- **Answer comparison**: solution sets are canonicalised (affine families via row reduction, lone free parameters absorbed). Known limitation: two different non-affine parametrisations of the same set (e.g. `a*b*x` vs `c*x`) are not recognised as equal.
- **Extra move `div`**: explicit division by a proven-nonzero constant exists mainly so the "divide by something not proven nonzero" trap can be tested; the normaliser performs legal divisions itself.
- **Size caps** (rows per case, monomials per identity, term size, open cases) reject moves that blow up. They limit what can be proven, never what is sound.
- **Model-check fuzzing** (`alphafe/modelcheck.py`, `tests/test_modelcheck.py`, `scripts/fuzz.py`): random accepted moves are tested against known solutions of the seed problems. It is a test tool and is never used to accept a proof.
- **Free moves**: the search environment (`alphafe/env.py`) applies conclude, cauchy, inj, surj and cancel automatically after every proposed move, for the baseline and the network alike. They need no creative input. The network therefore only chooses substitutions, case splits, preimages and `onto`. Free moves are still ordinary proof moves and are re-checked.
- **"Checker calls"** counts proposed moves only (accepted or rejected), not the free moves that follow them.
- **Brute force uses iterative widening** (tiny pool deep, then larger pools). Plain BFS is kept as an option and both are reported; widening is the stronger control.
- **Answer canary**: if a checked proof yields a solution set different from a recorded answer, evaluation raises `AnswerMismatch` instead of scoring it.
- **Baseline isolation**: `tests/test_isolation.py` fails if any training module imports `alphafe.baseline`. The baseline is not used to label, filter or order training problems.
- **Synthetic problems**: "random combination of f-expressions = its value under a planted function". The planted function is one solution; the full set is whatever a checked proof says. Problems the free moves alone finish are dropped as trivial.
- **Curriculum tiers are structural**: tier 1/2 = no nested f, small/large; tier 3/4 = nested f, small/large (`generate.tier_of`). Tiers say nothing about solvability; many tier 3-4 problems have no proof in the move language at all.
- **Held-out split**: about 10% of generated problems, chosen by a hash of the canonical equation (invariant under renaming variables and scaling). Seed and real problems are excluded from generation by the same hash.
- **Tokens**: prefix notation for all expressions; identities are shown as polynomials "= 0"; one-variable identities are shown once, not once per variable name. Network moves: `SUB`, `SPLIT`, `SPLITNZ`, `PRE`, `ONTO`. Terms the network writes are capped at 12 tokens.
- **Model**: encoder-decoder transformer, d=192, 4 encoder + 2 decoder layers, 2.5M parameters, value head on the mean-pooled encoder output. Apple M-series GPU (MPS).
- **MPS shape bucketing**: state sequences are padded to multiples of 64 tokens, move sequences to multiples of 8, sampling batches to powers of two. Without this, MPS compiles a graph per distinct shape and runs out of memory within minutes.
- **Search modes**: "no backtracking" = commit to the highest-probability sampled proposal the checker accepts, budget 32 checker calls; "small search budget" = best-first on cumulative move log-probability, budget 128 calls. Eight proposals are sampled per expanded state.
- **Training signal**: solve examples from checked proofs (minimised by dropping moves whose removal still leaves a proof), hindsight examples (state + derived identity as goal -> the move that derived it), value labels (1 on successful paths, 0 for states of failed attempts; the 0 labels are noisy because failure depends on the budget).
- **Exploration**: 2 of the 8 proposals per state come from the uniform grammar policy during self-play (none during evaluation).
- **Evaluations are run from a pinned worktree** (`.worktrees/`, gitignored) when the working tree has moved on, so a result always corresponds to one commit.
- **Lemmas over Q added after the first failure analysis**: `cauchy` accepts a polynomial defect (f(x+y) = f(x) + f(y) + 2xy), and `quadratic` handles f(x+y) + f(x-y) = 2f(x) + 2f(y). Both are textbook results valid over Q only and are refused over R.
- **Unique root**: a polynomial equation in a single unknown quantity with exactly one root in the domain gives that root (f(x)^2 = 0 gives f(x) = 0). With two or more roots nothing is deduced (that would be the pointwise trap).
- **Temperature spread for proposals**: the eight proposals per state are sampled at temperatures 0.6 to 3.0 (`model.DIVERSE`) and ranked by the model's temperature-1 probability. Reason: the trained policy is nearly deterministic in many states, and eight samples at one temperature were eight copies of one move (the M5 collapse).
- **Commit when a case closes** (network best-first search and brute force): drop the rest of the frontier and continue from the state that closed a case. Exactly lossless among states from the same split; versus unsplit states it is a safe heuristic that may cost more moves.
- **Mirror augmentation**: every proof found in self-play is also learned with x and y exchanged, replayed through the checker so the mirrored (state, move) pairs are real. Reason: the network had learned "substitute for y".
- **Reported proofs are minimised** (`env.minimise`): creative moves whose removal still leaves a complete proof are dropped before a proof is printed or used as a training example.
- **Frozen held-out set**: `benchmarks/synthetic/heldout.jsonl` (800 problems). Regenerating at evaluation time would let checker changes shift the set, because the generator drops problems the free moves solve.
- **The seed benchmark is a development set** from M6 on: fixes were chosen by looking at seed failures. Four three-variable problems (s47-s50) were added after E3, so later seed tables are out of 50.
- **Noise reporting**: trained-network rows are repeated over sampling seeds (`alphafe eval --seeds N`) and the range is reported; checker calls are compared on the problems both solvers solve.
- **Subterm exploration is optional and off by default** (`TrainConfig.n_subterm`): proposing substitution terms taken from the current identities did not improve results (RESULTS E5). It is a hand-made heuristic for self-play only, separate from the brute-force baseline's fixed pool, and is never used at evaluation time.
- **Job queues for long runs**: training and evaluation jobs are chained in a detached shell script so that one follows another on the GPU, and the package code is left untouched while a queue runs so each result corresponds to one commit.
- **Python versions actually tested**: everything runs on 3.12 (including a clean clone into a fresh venv). The torch-free tests also pass on 3.10, so nothing uses syntax newer than 3.11. Python 3.11 itself was not run: it is not installed on the development machine and downloading an interpreter was avoided (venv-only rule).
- **`alphafe eval` without a checkpoint** skips the trained-network rows and still runs the untrained network, the uniform policy and brute force, so a fresh clone can evaluate the real benchmark.
- **Trained weights are published as GitHub Release assets, not committed.** The repository stays small and the brief's rule against committing checkpoints holds. The published files are weights-only exports (`scripts/export_checkpoint.py`, 10 MB instead of 30 MB), and `load_model` uses `weights_only=True` so a downloaded file cannot run code. The download is a manual `curl`; `solve` and `eval` never touch the network.
- **The real benchmark is compiled under written rules** (`benchmarks/real/INSTRUCTIONS.md`): every problem needs an opened source, the selection rule is fixed before the solver runs, and skipped problems are listed with reasons. `scripts/validate_bench.py` checks what can be checked mechanically. A real problem that equals a seed or held-out problem is left out, because the seed set was tuned on and the held-out file is frozen.
- **README split**: the move reference, the soundness rules and the module map moved to `docs/DESIGN.md`; the README keeps the example, setup, results and scope.
- **Real problems that are no longer unseen are flagged, not removed.** Eleven of the real problems were also in the hand-picked study behind issue #99, where the solver was run on them and proposals were written around them. They carry `"seen_in_development": true`, as does `swiss-mo-2006-1`, which is seed s31 after the change of variables x -> (x+y)/2, y -> (x-y)/2. Results are to be given with and without them. Removing them would raise the score, since most are known failures. USAMO 2002/4 stays out of the real set: the solver was run on it before the rule that would have selected it existed, and it joins the seed set with the move that issue #2 asks for. Two problems with exponents above 2000 (China TST 2021 III-5, Korean MO 2015/1) had been skipped only because the validator did not finish on them; they are back in the file, 137 problems in all, and count as unsolved while the checker refuses to load them.
- **CI tests Python 3.11, 3.12 and 3.13** (`.github/workflows/tests.yml`): the suite without torch on all three, and with CPU-only torch on 3.12. This replaces the earlier note that 3.11 had never been run. Training and `alphafe eval` stay out of CI.
- **The first run on the real benchmark was made under a protocol committed beforehand** (RESULTS.md, M7): command, weights, rows and tables fixed in advance, one run from a clean tree, harness changes shown to reproduce an earlier run exactly. Later milestones get one run on the real benchmark each, after their configuration has been chosen on the seed and held-out sets only.
- **An equation the checker cannot load counts as unsolved.** The size caps are not lifted for the original equation, because substitutions into it would build identities of the same size.
- **Routine substitutions belong to the environment, not to the search** (M8). Opening substitutions, the swap, closed instances and witnesses are applied after every proposed move, through the checker and into the proof. An instance is taken only if it mentions no f(...) that is not already present; without that rule the step feeds itself. The generator still filters with the free moves alone, so that the frozen held-out file and the pools of published weights keep their definition.
- **Lemmas with syntactic preconditions were preferred to sign-restricted variables** for "x^2 takes every non-negative value" (`sqadd`, `sqlin`): about 60 lines of trusted code with written proofs and traps, against a new kind of variable in the checker. On the real benchmark they reach one solved problem so far.
- **Weights are chosen on held-out tiers 3 and 4 at 128 calls**, and a milestone's configuration on the seed and held-out sets; the real benchmark is run once per milestone. The 12 real problems flagged as seen in development may be used during development.
- **Mixing brute-force pool moves into the network search is available but off** (`BestFirstSearch(systematic=n)`): within noise at 128 calls on the development sets, one or two seed problems at 2,000. Training never uses it, since the brute-force pool is the control.
- **A development suite, separate from the real benchmark** (`benchmarks/dev`, M9): 20 hand-picked harder problems plus the 12 real problems already flagged as seen. It may be looked at and tuned on. Candidates that were twins of an unflagged real problem or of a seed problem were dropped without being run, and a test repeats that check. The real benchmark answers "how good is it"; this suite answers "what next".
- **Speed changes to the checker must be exact.** A change that is meant only to save time has to give the same proofs, checker calls and node counts as before on the 883 development problems (brute force, 600 calls) and the same solved, calls and proof length on the stored 2,000-call run. A cheaper variant that changed two results out of 706 was not merged (issue #109).
- **Searches mix in proposals built from the page** (`model.SUBTERM_PROPOSALS = 2` of 8): the trained policy gives substitutions whose term involves f almost no probability, and no amount of sampling finds them. This reverses the earlier note that subterm proposals are never used at evaluation time; the "no backtracking" row is still the network alone. Because of it every big-budget network row is reported next to the same search with random weights.
- **Work that would be refused anyway is not done** (`checker.WORK_TERMS`, `env.INSTANCE_WORK`): expanding a substitution stops with "identity too large" once a multiplication would exceed 1,000 term pairs. Like the other caps this is a termination guard: it can refuse a legal move, never accept an illegal one.
- **The opening substitutions were widened instead of the search** (M9): 1, -1 and f of a variable for x and y, on top of the zeros and diagonals. A search branches on such substitutions; applied together at the start they accumulate in one state. The cap on identities per case went from 40 to 80 for it. Candidates were screened with brute force on the 883 development problems and adopted only if they paid at equal CPU time as well as at equal calls; the same openings on derived identities did not.
- **Self-play runs in worker processes on CPU cores** (`TrainConfig.workers`) while gradient steps run on the GPU, with at most `max_steps_per_iter` steps while a round is out. Nothing else heavy may run on the machine during training: a run is limited by wall-clock time, and in run m2 side jobs slowed two rounds to 17 and 21 minutes.
- **The checker is tested against solutions that are not polynomials** (`tests/test_nonpoly.py`, issue #96) before any change that lets it accept more. `cancel` on constant arguments was the first such change after the suite existed.
